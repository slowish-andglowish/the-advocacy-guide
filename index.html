A guide for parents

Yes, Your Child Can and Should Enjoy School

Neurodivergent children, and children with common learning disabilities, still experience anxiety, failure and exclusion at school in large numbers. It does not have to be that way. If your child is autistic, has ADHD, or has a learning disorder, their school has a legal duty to adjust how it teaches them so that school works for them. Most parents are never told this.

Written by a parent, for parents, at NSW schools. General information, not legal advice.

Most children with neurodivergence and learning disabilities can thrive in mainstream schooling when this approach is in place (Hehir et al. 2016, 280 studies; Disability Royal Commission 2023). The law exists to make this happen, in every school, in every sector.

Start here: a neuroaffirming view

Two ways to see your child: as a mind that works differently, or as a set of deficits. The first is the one the school is required to use.

How it feels, and what that tells you

Confusion can be evidence. In a school with a neuroaffirming approach, you, your child and the school work together, curious and experimental, on how your child's physical, learning and social environment is designed, so that their education goes well.

If you cannot describe that process at your child's school, it may not be there. If you also feel worn down, unwelcome, and as though the fault is yours, that may be the experience of discrimination the Act and the Standards were written to prevent.
